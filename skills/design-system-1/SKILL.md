# Design system 1

Design system 1 (`/Users/joachim/0001/Design system 1`, source in `DS1/`) is the framework every 0001 product is drawn on. Products carry a copy or a fork of it (see the 0001 brief), so these rules hold even where the package is not imported. Client sites keep the client's own design unless 0001 is designing for them.

## Design decisions

Cut taglines, helper text and secondary labels. If a screen needs them to make sense, fix the layout.

Keep data current automatically on entry, after changes and when returning to the page or reconnecting. Never add Reload or Refresh buttons, including icon-only equivalents. Preserve focused fields, unsaved edits and pending writes during background updates; show failures inline and recover reads automatically without replaying writes.

Always make the best decision that elevates all three: the user experience (UX), developer experience (DX), and agent experience (AX), without breaking anything.

## Web interface

Use the Lit `ds-*` components for web controls, including plain HTML pages: a stylesheet link loads the foundations and a module script registers the elements. React uses the same components through adapters, which Sync 1's `components` export copies with them; a product without React leaves them out with `"react": false` beside its `"components"`. Libraries customize their public tokens. CLASSless and the vanilla stylesheet are retired. Native classes and page helpers mentioned below describe existing implementation/consumer compatibility; select the Lit component for new work. Keep semantic HTML inside/slotted into controls and for prose. Swift, Kotlin and email keep their platform renderers.

## Non-modal apps

Keep the working page usable. Edit values inline; open small choices in an anchored dropdown; use a page or a non-modal side panel for larger work. Link follows this rule alongside Money and Ezo. A date opens the compact DS1 calendar dropdown, with no outer border or padding, no gap from its trigger or between its control groups, and single-day/range selection and Clear; a status opens DS1 Select. Neither gets a centred form with a backdrop or a separate Cancel/Save ceremony.

Compose existing DS1 controls. For a side panel or sheet, set `ds-overlay.nonModal = true`, `holdScroll = false` and `blockOutside = false`; choose `type="drawer"`, `"sheet"` or an anchored `"popover"` explicitly. Keep the surrounding page interactive, with no dimming backdrop, focus trap or page scroll lock. Full-screen document viewing is a deliberate viewer mode. Keep dismissal, focus return, unsaved work and pending saves safe; a failed automatic edit restores the accepted value and shows an actionable error. Confirm destructive actions inline or in an anchored non-modal confirmation before executing them.

Do not introduce a centred modal or a blocking browser `alert`, `confirm` or `prompt` in an app. Native operating-system file/permission pickers remain platform-owned. Legacy modal APIs remain compatibility APIs, not the default pattern for new or revised app flows. Verify keyboard/outside dismissal, background interaction and a failed save before calling a migrated flow complete.

## Propagating shared design

### Direct editing surfaces

For composition editing, make the space or boundary itself the interaction target. Use a quiet measurement label and an accessible precise control alongside it. Do not add dot-grid drag grips or floating plus-button rails beside blocks; Joachim rejected that pattern on 22 October 2026. Embedded editors must disable that chrome through their supported options, not CSS that reaches into their internals. Explore and test the direct interaction before propagating it to existing products.

Pointer movement follows the hand continuously; snap to the grid only on release, with a short, interruptible settling motion. For a spacing measurement at the gap midpoint, change the gap by twice the pointer displacement (adjusted for device scale), so the midpoint stays under the pointer. Keep the label anchored to the actual measurement, never floating independently. Preserve that geometry in both axes; a dragged outer boundary follows at full speed. Keep text crisp, use transform/opacity for the settling animation, respect reduced motion, and keep keyboard adjustments immediate. Read the `animate` and `emil-design-eng` skills when implementing this motion.

Linked component previews (`editing.linked-preview`): when consistency is enabled, a spacing drag updates the corresponding gap and required container growth in every matching component in the same preview frame, before release. Keep each instance’s text independent. Preview leaves authored content and history untouched; release commits the linked design once, and cancellation restores every instance. Verify with a pointer held down, including linked instances with different text lengths and with consistency disabled.

Interaction ownership (`editing.gesture-ownership`): selection is persistent state, not an exclusive editing mode. Marquee selection must leave spacing handles available; explicit spacing and resize targets win over background selection or reordering. An active pointer gesture must not open passive hover tools as it crosses other elements. Decide gesture ownership before competing adapters handle the press. Genuine open contexts temporarily pause geometry controls without changing saved preferences; deliberate keyboard access still works. Coalesce marquee movement to one paint per animation frame, cache hit-test bounds until content/layout/scroll changes, and keep selection outside authored history. Release commits a content edit once; cancellation restores its previous state. Verify gesture handoffs, additive selection, scrolling and cancellation with actual pointer input, and assert that marquee movement does not rebuild the editor or repeatedly measure unchanged content.

Text must never render below its DS1 role size, including in interactive component previews. A preview uses the selected library's actual text and control sizing; reduce its surrounding padding or reflow the specimen instead of shrinking it with zoom, transforms or smaller fonts. Film-kit compositions are the explicit exception: their camera and composition may scale the design.

### Source-first workflow

1. Identify the canonical component, token or interaction in `DS1/`. Move a reusable improvement discovered in a consumer into that source first, including its documented measure or behavior.
2. Inventory applicable consumers from the System implementation guide, Sync 1 manifests and lockfiles, and targeted source searches, including copied or forked implementations and native ports. Record the scope in the work item's note.
3. Commit the shared source, then copy it through each consumer's approved Sync 1 exports. Preserve product-owned code and reconcile legacy forks deliberately; remove superseded local design overrides. Keep content, domain behavior, intentional platform conventions and client branding in the consumer.
4. Run source and consumer checks, follow each project's release procedure and deployment policy, and verify released assets over HTTP. Browser verification follows the project's brief and the user's request.
5. Finish with an explicit account of consumers migrated and released, consumers with no applicable usage, intentional exceptions and blocked consumers. Source checks and copied files alone do not establish live adoption. Keep unfinished propagation visible in the work item.

## Sizing modes: fill and hug

Name the modes **fill** and **hug**. Fill occupies the available width; hug sizes to the control’s content. Select triggers and date-picker triggers always use hug, never fill: no full-row stretch, growing flex item, fixed wide variant or width token that expands them. Status tags remain tags when clickable; do not wrap them in a filled select face or add an arrow. Their anchored menu still provides the choices.

| A card | **Its size in whole squares**, padded one square: its name on one square in the muted ink, a display title on lines of a square and a half, a picture **on the card's foot**, a square below the words at least, so the pictures of cards side by side (one height in their row, one aspect ratio) stand on one level; **a picture is never rounded where it meets the card**: square on the foot, a picture's corner only free inside the card towards the words; with `media="bleed"` it runs on to the end edge too, with no corners of its own; the pointer tints the card and nothing in it moves; a palette pastel as its fill (`tone`), a card's corners (`--ds-radius-card`); with `href` one press target | `ds-card`, `.ds-card` |
| A page's columns | **Whole squares**: navigation 12, a list beside its detail 16, an inspector 12, a reading or settings column 32, a single task 16, overview tiles 12. Two squares between columns, one between areas, one around the page; one column below 30 squares of width. A `column` or `centered` page stands on a whole square (the room beside it rounds down to squares) and `measure="N"` sets its width in squares. `--ds-page-bleed` (Swift `DS1Page(bleed:)`) lets the content stand that far outside the column on each side, the column and the grid kept: a phone's lists take 4px (Money) A `column` or `centered` page stands what it holds one square apart (`--ds-page-flow`), so a page writes no layout CSS of its own: a row of buttons is a `ds-row`, a form a `ds-form`. | `ds-page` layouts `sidebar`, `split`, `inspector`, `column`, `centered`, `tiles` |

### Where words and pictures stand in a square (the sixteen)

Ezo's rule, the one to apply before any other alignment, so text and icons never have to be corrected afterwards. **In a square, words, pictures and controls take the lower sixteen, with the spare four above them.** A 14px line of text is set on a 16px line box (`--ds-type-lineheight-default`, Ezo `--type-lineheight-default`), and that box stands against the foot of its 20px square: the capitals are then 3px inside the sixteen at both ends (cap top 7px below the square's top line, baseline 3px above its bottom line), and descenders hang over the bottom line. A 16px icon stands on the same sixteen, so the capitals are centred on it. A control (a button, a chip, a switch, 16px high, `--ds-control-height`) stands there too. Centring in the twenty instead is wrong: the words sit 2px higher than in every Ezo row, and an icon placed at the foot is then 2px lower than the words.

- **A row** (flex or grid, one square high): `align-items: flex-end` (grid: `end`) and `line-height: calc(16px * var(--sf))` on the row; a `min-height` of one square keeps a 16px control from shrinking the row. Everything in it, words, amount, icon, button, lands on the sixteen.
- **A block line** (a heading, a date, a total, a label on its own): `padding-top: 4px` (scaled) and `line-height: 16px` (scaled), so the line is still one square.
- **A square with a rule at its foot** (a table cell, a day heading, a tab bar): three spare above the sixteen, then the 1px rule: `padding-top: 3px` with the 16px line, or `align-items: flex-end` in the 19px content box. Never `line-height: 19px`.
- **Two lines in two squares** (a name over a small second line): each line is its own four and sixteen; the second line's spare drops to three when the pair ends on a rule.
- **Across:** a picture takes the first sixteen of its square (flush with the square's edge, not centred in it), and the words begin on the next square, so the square's spare four stand between the picture and the words. Words without a picture begin on the square's edge too (a pressed row pads them 4px, see the table).
- Design system 1's own rows do this since 17 October 2026: `.ds-row` and `ds-row` end their contents on the row's bottom line with a 16px line, the sidebar's tree rows (`.ds-sidebar`, `ds-sidebar tree`) too with their arrow and icon drawn on the sixteen, and `.ds-table` cells set the sixteen against the rule; a product copying them gets it for free. Since 18 October 2026 every button face (`ds-button` in each `control` mode, the React `Button`, `.ds-button`) is one control high and stands at the foot of a line (`vertical-align: bottom`); a slotted button keeps a one-square box with its face at the foot (an icon's face on the square's first sixteen), and a menu entry, a row high, sets its words and icon on the lower sixteen; never give a button a face a row high to centre its words. `ds-text` already sets the 16px line box, so a `ds-text` in a flex-end row needs nothing more; a native `span` needs the row's `line-height`. Small type (10px) inline in a line keeps `line-height: 1`. Prose that wraps stays on 20px lines (one square a line) and is the exception to the sixteen.
- Check it as Ezo does: a 14px capital's top is 7px below the row's top grid line and its baseline 3px above the next; a 16px icon's bottom edge is on that next line.

Items stack edge to edge with no gap, as an Ezo list does. Lists, table rows, cells and their headers use neutral or transparent backgrounds by default, never automatic accent fills. Colour is opt-in for a meaningful state, not decoration across an entire list or table. Positions snap to the grid: anything a person can move lands on whole squares. Before calling a design done, measure it in the browser (an element's width and height must be whole squares minus the 1px correction) instead of judging by eye.

## Tags

`ds-tag` is a noninteractive category or status label: one control high, 4px scaled padding on each side, and the shared small corner. Like a button it stands on the lower sixteen of its square: at the foot of a line, and in a row that stretches it to a square its fill stays a control high at the row's foot, so its words share a baseline with a 16px line of text beside it (since 26 October 2026). Its width hugs its text without rounding to squares, an intentional exception for compact labels. Use `--ds-tag-accent` for a pale fill and coloured text. App navigation uses Tag; Badge retains its grid-sized geometry.

## Text and prose

- **Interface copy** (labels, messages, titles in an app) is `ds-text`: `tone` (`muted`, `title`, `warning`, `danger`), `size` (`small`, `heading`, `display`), `strong` and `wrap` work for a translation key as for `literal` text (what a person wrote is always `literal`, never a key; `ds-accordion literal` does the same for its summary and details). A heading is `role="heading"` with `aria-level`. A page without scripts uses the classes in `DS1/1-root/text.css` on native text: `ds-text`, `ds-text--small`, `--heading`, `--display`, `--strong`, `--muted`, `--title`, `--warning`, `--danger`. `one.css` and `controls.css` both import it.
- **Long-form and marketing writing** stays native headings, paragraphs, lists and tables inside an element marked `data-ds-prose`, drawn by `DS1/1-root/prose.css`; a part that draws itself inside it carries `data-ds-not-prose`. Never restyle prose elements by hand, and never wrap each paragraph in `ds-text`. Both count as Design system 1's styles.
- Every line of text is one square (`--ds-unit`); a display title (`h1` in prose) takes two. One square stands between two blocks of writing and two before a heading. Code is 13px in the code face on the same lines.
- Failure and loss text is `--ds-danger-color` (Red for blood on light paper, Tuned red on dark), never a raw red. Text that asks for care is `--ds-warning-color` (Orange's dim on light paper, Orange on dark), never raw Orange, which is too light for text on light paper.

## The rest of the language

- Colours, typefaces and tokens come from `DS1/1-root/one.css` (or the product's copy, e.g. Ezo's `client/design/1-root/one.css`). Use the tokens, never raw values.
- Words sit straight on the paper, with no cards or shadows around content.
- **Never slant boxes on screen.** Keep every box upright, with horizontal top and bottom edges and vertical sides: cards, panels, receipts, screenshots, device frames and other rectangular mockups. Never add decorative rotation, skew, perspective tilt or an angled animation to them, including in landing-page illustrations. Translation, scaling and opacity changes must preserve this upright orientation.
- Sentence case in interface copy, never all caps.
- Arrows (→ ← ↑ ↓ ⇒ ↩ …) are always Inter, in every typeface, code included: "Inter Arrows" (`fonts/Inter-Arrows.woff2`, Inter's arrows alone, limited by `unicode-range`) leads every `--typeface-*` token. A new typeface token or font stack starts with it; never draw an arrow in GT America, Iosevka or a system font.
- Code is Iosevka Extended (`--typeface-mono`), loaded from `fonts/Iosevka-Regular.woff2`, with a monospace fallback, never the browser's serif.
- The faces are loaded once, by `DS1/1-root/fonts.css` (the `@font-face` rules and the `--typeface-*` tokens; `one.css` imports it). A page without `one.css` links `fonts.css` (Sync 1: `root` with `"styles": ["fonts.css"]`, or `fonts` with `"stylesheet": true`); never write a product's own `@font-face` rules for DS1's faces. No stylesheet a page always loads fetches from another host.
- Japanese and Chinese name Noto Sans and then the reader's own faces (Hiragino, PingFang, Yu Gothic, Microsoft YaHei and JhengHei, Noto Sans CJK), so nothing downloads by default. A product that shows them and wants Noto Sans adds the opt-in `cjk.css` (Sync 1 `"cjk": true`; from Google Fonts, sliced by `unicode-range`, with `font-synthesis-weight: none`); a page whose security policy keeps other hosts out leaves it out.
- Motion animates `transform` and `opacity` only.
- Web controls use Lit elements, including in ordinary HTML pages. Load the shared stylesheet and module script as shown at https://designsystem1.com/start-here/quick-start/. `controls.css` and the native-control Sync 1 option remain compatibility dependencies for existing copies, not another supported interface.
- A client's look lives in a published design library (the libraries app), written in `--one-*` tokens; the brand stays the client's. Publishing passes those values to the components' own tokens (`DS1/1-root/library-tokens.ts`) and gives links marked `data-library-button` the library's button faces, so never map `--one-*` to `--ds-*` or rewrite a library's selectors in a product. A product pins a version through Sync 1's `library` export (`"id"`, `"version"`), not `latest.css`, and loads it after DS1's stylesheets in the same or a later cascade layer. https://designsystem1.com/libraries/
- The same sheet draws the pieces: `ds-field` (a label above or, with `--beside`, four squares beside a native control, `ds-field__hint` muted and `ds-field__error` in the danger colour, all on one-square rows), `ds-avatar` (`--large`, `--square`, `--pair`, `--outlined`, `--pending`), `ds-badge` (`--icon`), `ds-toggle` on a `.ds-button` with `aria-pressed`, `ds-icon` (`--small`, `--large`, the file in `--ds-icon`) and `ds-drop` (`--compact`) on a `<label>` around `<input type="file">`, which also makes a `label.ds-button` the file picker's button. Never rebuild a field label, avatar, badge, icon mask or drop zone in a product.
- A setting that is on or off is a switch (`role="switch"`); an action that stays on, such as showing a panel or muting a track, is a toggle button (`aria-pressed`, `ds-toggle`); one of several choices is a segmented control (`ds-tabs` with `role="group"`). There is one switch, `<ds-switch>` (`ds-switch` without Lit, `.ds1Switch`, `DS1Switch`): a pill with a white round thumb, grey off and the darker green on, whatever the accent; never a square one. Dense web control panels may opt into `compact monochrome`: a smaller pill within a two-square by one-square button, ink/paper on and quiet grey off, preserving readable labels, focus, disabled state and keyboard activation. Normal and native platform switches keep their established geometry and green on-state. A `ds-cycle` whose face shows only a value (a size, a count) takes `label`, the setting's name as a key, so it is announced as "Size: 40"; never write the name into each value. A `ds-button` whose face is a value takes `label` as its accessible name.
- A row, tile or card that opens something is one press target, never a `<div>` with a click handler or a row of separate links: `<ds-row href>` (a destination) or `<ds-row interactive>` (an action), or `a.ds-row` / `button.ds-row` without Lit; a row with controls of its own keeps them in the `end` slot, or beside a `.ds-row__target`, never nested in the row's link. `ds-row-press` is cancelable for routing. Navigation marks the page being shown with `current` (`aria-current="page"`), drawn as the sidebar's current row. Nested things a person opens and chooses from (files, groups, a page's parts) are the sidebar's tree, `<ds-sidebar tree>` (`role="tree"`, rows `role="treeitem"`, one Tab stop, `sidebar-select`), not its navigation: the navigation is the application's places, each row its own Tab stop. https://designsystem1.com/3-unit/row/ and https://designsystem1.com/3-unit/sidebar/#tree
- `ds-list` is a list to assistive technology (`role="list"`, each element directly inside it `role="listitem"`); never add those roles by hand. A list used only for layout takes `role="none"`. Every element hides with `hidden`.
- A `ds-composer` highlight is a tint of the words; one that must show its colour (the accent on a change, a colour value) takes `fill: true`, the colour behind the words, never a hand-drawn overlay.
- On phones and tablets every measure is multiplied by `--sf` (1 on a computer; the screen width over 280 on a phone, between 0.75 and 2). Write lengths as units or `calc(Npx * var(--sf))`, never bare pixels, except hairlines. A computer stays at 1 however narrow its window; a phone or tablet is a mobile browser or a touch screen whose shorter side is at most 820px. Put the scale on with `startScaling()` from `DS1/0-face/scaling.ts` before the first render (`snap: "layout"` or `"pixel"` where rows must stay on drawn lines), never a hand-written setter or a width breakpoint. A phone drawn on a page (a prototype's frame) scales to its own width with `startScaling({ surface: element })`, or `surface: true` for every `data-ds-surface` element, on any device and with the same snapping (it sets `--sf` and `--ds-sf` on the surface), never `calculateScalingFactor` wired by hand. https://designsystem1.com/1-root/scaling/
- A destination is a link, an action a button. Every link is a real `<a href>`, never navigation from a click handler: `.ds-link` in running text (the text's ink, a hairline underline, sentence case), and a link drawn as a button is `<ds-button href>`, React `ButtonLink` or `a.ds-button`, identical to the button in every face and state. A disabled link has no `href` and keeps `role="link"` and `aria-disabled="true"`. https://designsystem1.com/2-core/link/
- Offer Light, Dark and System (the default, following the system live), stored as one choice. Inline `DS1/0-face/theme-bootstrap.js` in the head so the first paint is already themed; it marks the root with the `light-theme` or `dark-theme` class (canonical; `data-theme` mirrors it) and `color-scheme`. Change it with `setThemeMode()` or `<ds-cycle type="theme">`, never a page's own pre-paint script. A product that keeps Light for first-time visitors sets `themeDefault: "light"` on `window.DS_ONE_PREFERENCES` before the head script. A page without the components takes `theme.ts` through Sync 1's `root` export, `"modules": ["theme"]`. https://designsystem1.com/1-root/theming/
- Offer Square, Rounded and Circular as the reader's shape, as a theme: `<ds-cycle type="shape">`, `setShape()` from `DS1/0-face/shape.ts`, put on the root as `data-ds-shape` before the first paint by the same head script (`theme-bootstrap.js`); rounded draws squircles where the browser can. A product sets `shapeDefault` on `window.DS_ONE_PREFERENCES` for a reader who has chosen none, and never writes its own shape setting or corner overrides. https://designsystem1.com/1-root/shape/
- Page scripts from `0-face/` travel in Sync 1's `root` export (`"modules"`); `"scriptsTo"`, a path from `"to"`, puts them apart from the stylesheets (scripts compiled from `src/`, stylesheets published from `public/`), never a product's own copy or shim. https://designsystem1.com/1-root/scaling/

## Carousels

Slides that move sideways are `<ds-carousel label>` (React `Carousel`, `DS1/react/carousel.ts`; without Lit `.ds-carousel` from `DS1/1-root/carousel.css` and `startCarousels()` from `DS1/0-face/carousel.ts`, Sync 1's root `"modules": ["carousel"]`), with `buttons` for previous and next (the `left` and `right` icons) and the dots below. It is a region described as a carousel and named by `label`, each slide a group labelled "2 of 5", the slide in view announced politely, and it jumps at once under reduced motion. It fires `change` when a person moves. Page dots alone are `<ds-pagedots count active>`: buttons in a group, `select` when one is picked (cancelable), arrows, Home and End. A strip of cards to browse, of any widths, is the same carousel with `slide-width="auto"` (native `data-slides="auto"`): `bleed` (`end` runs on to the window's end edge while the first card keeps the column, `both` to both edges), `loop` (held three times over, copies inert and hidden, no dots), `autoplay` (opt-in, for a strip that shows off, never for something a person must read or act on; always with a pause button, held on hover, touch, focus, out of view and under reduced motion), `free`. A finger, a pen or a trackpad scrolls the strip and a mouse drags it (`dragTrack`, snapping held off while it drags); a press that ends a drag or a swipe never presses the slide it ends on, so cards that open something can be swiped across. A carousel never moves on its own without `autoplay`. Never build a product's own scroll-snap strip, endless rail, dots or slide counter. https://designsystem1.com/3-unit/carousel/

## Cards

Several things shown at once, of different sizes, are a `<ds-bento>` of cards (`.ds-bento` without Lit), half a square apart; never a product's own bento grid. https://designsystem1.com/3-unit/bento/

The things in a strip are `<ds-card>` (`.ds-card`): `href` for one link, `tone` a palette colour's pastel (or `ink`), `width` and `height` in squares, slots `label`, `title`, default and `media` (`center`, `bleed`). Never build a product's own card tile. https://designsystem1.com/2-core/card/

## Toasts

**Save feedback:** Never show persistent save-status text or badges in the interface ("Saved", "Saving…", "All changes saved", or equivalents). Use the shared Sonner toast only when confirmation is useful, such as after an explicit save or recovery from a failed save. Routine autosave stays silent. Preserve pending-write protection, unsaved-change safeguards and actionable errors; do not replace them with a permanent status label.

Message is one component with inline, banner, toast and assistant forms (`ds-message`, React `Message` from `DS1/react/message`). A toast is `<ds-message form="toast">` and `message()` from `DS1/2-core/ds-message.ts`, based on Sonner: Sonner's calls, options and stacking behaviour, drawn as the system's label: an ink chip one control high per line in the tooltip's colours, padded 4px, no line or shadow, actions as underlined words, as wide as its words up to sixteen squares, the pile advancing one square per toast and standing one square from the edge. In the window the pile stands in the browser's top layer (a manual popover, raised again for each new toast), so an open sheet, drawer or modal never covers it. Toasts persist until dismissed and show a close button by default; a finite duration is an explicit opt-in. An error is announced assertively. Never build a product's own toast or notification stack. Existing copies may import the same `toast()` from `DS1/0-face/feedback.ts`: its page toaster draws the same stack with `ds-toast`'s stylesheet, and a `<ds-toast>` in the page takes every toast, so a page never shows two stacks.

## Menus: Select and Context

- **Every menu that opens from a button is a Select**, `<ds-select>`: around a native `<select>` it is a field whose choices DS1 lists in place of the platform's picker (options may carry `data-details`, `data-shortcut` and `data-icon`; `<optgroup>` heads a group; `native` keeps the platform's picker); without one it is the action menu the button named by `for` opens, its entries `ds-button menu-item` rows, separators and labels; without an opener it is a list in place. React `Select` from `react/select` is both, and takes `items`, entries as data (label, details, icon, shortcut, href, danger, disabled, checked, keepOpen, onSelect, nested `items`; `{ heading }`, `{ separator: true }`). Never a product's own dropdown, popover menu, positioning or menu keyboard. https://designsystem1.com/3-unit/select/
- **A menu that opens where the pointer is, is a Context**, `<ds-context for>` (React `Context`, `ContextTrigger`, `ContextContent`, `ContextItem`… from `react/context`), in Ezo's gap face. `context-selector` delegates one menu to a long list's rows. Choosing a row closes it unless the row says `data-keep-open`; `pending` holds the rows and keeps it open while a change is awaited; `refuse(row)` shows a refused change on its row. A page without Lit draws the same face with the native `.ds-context` (rows its buttons, `hr` a group gap, `ds-context__item--danger`, `__label`, `__shortcut`): Select's menu is light, Context's dark. The formatting bar over selected words (or the actions over a selected thing) is the same Context with `bar`: `<ds-context bar>` opened by `openOver(rect)` over the selection's rectangle, its rows across (← and →), the caret left in the writing (React `ContextContent bar anchor`; native `.ds-context--bar`). Never a product's own context menu, long press, gap-style rows or selection toolbar. https://designsystem1.com/3-unit/context/

## Table of contents

A page's section links are `<ds-table-of-contents>` (React `TableOfContents` from `react/table-of-contents.ts`), a Unit: twelve squares wide by default, a title on one row, one square before the links, each link one square high, nested sections a whole square further in. A cut label shows its full words in the shared tooltip. Other links use the muted ink; the current section uses the sharp blue, customizable with `--ds-toc-current-color`. Supply native links with `data-depth`, or `items` with `href`, `label` and `depth`. The Unit tracks the actual scrolling container, marks the current section with `aria-current="location"`, and focuses a section when followed. The page owns right/left placement, sticky positioning and responsive visibility. https://designsystem1.com/3-unit/table-of-contents/

## Dates

A date or a range a person chooses is Calendar, `<ds-calendar picker name label>` (a field that opens the small month) or `<ds-calendar selectable>` (a month in place); never a product's own date picker, a `<input type="date">` drawn by hand or a date library's picker. There is one way to choose, Notion's: the first day pressed is the value, the next ends a range from it (before or after; the same day twice keeps one day), the press after starts again, and in the picker leaving after the first keeps one day. No single-day or range modes to set. The value is ISO 8601, `2026-10-21`, `2026-10-21/2026-10-24`, with a time (`2026-10-21T09:00`) when Include time is on, and the picker takes part in its form. https://designsystem1.com/3-unit/calendar/#picker

This holds in admin and settings screens too, and in a product drawn with the native classes alone: it takes the Calendar through the `components` export (`"components": ["calendar"]`, `"react": false`, with `lit`) and makes `<ds-calendar picker>`, never a native date field because the rest of the page has no Lit (the site engine's booking checkout, `app/booking.ts`, is the reference). A booking's days are `<ds-calendar compact selectable>` with `min`, `max` and `dayAllowed` (the days with room left): one day for a time on it, a check-in and check-out as one range for nights, never a booking page's own month grid. A season or any from–to is one range picker, its empty face the words for no limit ("All year"); a list of days, such as closed days, is its days as tags each with its close, then a picker and Add, which adds the day chosen or every day of the range.

How much happens on each day (commits, purchases, hours) is Calendar's `heat`, a contribution graph in a month: give the events a `count` and each day is lit in one of four steps of the accent; `heat-max` keeps the scale from month to month. Never a product's own heat map, intensity colours or contribution grid. https://designsystem1.com/3-unit/calendar/#heat

## Overlays, questions and copying text

Never call the browser's `alert()`, `confirm()` or `prompt()`, and never write a product's own copy-to-clipboard helper. For copy feedback import `copyText` from `DS1/0-face/feedback.ts`; the legacy `confirm`, `prompt` and `alert` exports are compatibility APIs, while app confirmations follow Non-modal apps above (Sync 1's `feedback` export): one import, plain TypeScript with no `?inline` or `?raw` import and no language runtime. Its default words read English, and the shared words in the page's language where the page loads the language module (copy `feedback` to the same folder as `languages`, or set `"languages": true` on it); `setFeedbackWords()` gives a product's own. Never add a plugin, a type declaration or a tsconfig exclusion to make the copy build.

- `confirm({ title, text, confirm, cancel, danger })` resolves to a boolean, `prompt({ title, label, value, placeholder, confirm, cancel })` to the text or `null`, `alert({ title, text })` once dismissed. They are drawn on the native `dialog.ds-overlay` frame from `controls.css`, with `.ds-button--secondary` Cancel and `.ds-button--primary` answer; their default words are the shared words (OK, Cancel) in the page's language.
- A destructive action names itself and sets `danger` (`confirm: "Delete", danger: true`): its words in the danger colour (`.ds-button--danger`), Cancel focused, no backdrop dismissal.
- `copyText(text, { toast })` copies with the Clipboard API (a hidden field where it is missing) and confirms with the shared word "Copied" in a toast; call it from the click that asks for it. A page that only copies imports it from `DS1/0-face/copy-text.ts`, which loads the page toaster only when it shows a toast.
- **Everything that stands over the page is an Overlay**, `<ds-overlay type>` (React `Overlay` and `OverlayContent type` from `react/overlay`): a legacy `modal` in the centre (the component’s compatibility default; apps choose a non-modal type explicitly), a `sheet` from the foot of the screen, a `drawer` at its `side` (`end` or `start`), a `popover` at an `anchor` point (modeless), or the whole `screen`. Never a product's own modal, panel, popover or full-screen layer. Its title stands on the sixteen, and its header keeps a square for the close only when there is one (`showClose={false}` gives the title the row). A question uses an anchored non-modal confirmation; copy feedback uses the page’s `<ds-toast>`. https://designsystem1.com/4-page/overlay/
- **Sheets and drawers** are Overlay's types: `type="sheet"` from the bottom (a phone's first choice), `type="drawer"` at the `side` `end` or `start` (React `OverlayContent type side`; native `.ds-overlay--sheet`, `--drawer` with `--start`, with `bindOverlaySwipe()` from `0-face/overlay-swipe.ts`, Sync 1 `root` `"modules": ["overlay-swipe"]`). Apps set `nonModal` and keep Escape and focus return while leaving the page interactive, without a backdrop or scroll lock. A finger swipes them away towards their edge: one to one after ten pixels, rubber-banded the other way, dismissed when the projected rest is past half their size, asking first with `ds-close-request` reason `"swipe"` (declined, it settles home). Content scrolled down, sideways strips, sliders and `data-ds-overlay-no-swipe` keep their touches. Never build a product's own bottom sheet, drawer, drag handle or swipe gesture, and never a drawer library's. The feedback module’s legacy questions are modal compatibility APIs; use the non-modal app pattern for product flows. https://designsystem1.com/4-page/overlay/#sheets-and-drawers

## Keyboard shortcuts

Write a shortcut once, `"mod+shift+d"`, and let `DS1/0-face/keys.ts` do the rest (Sync 1 `root` `"modules": ["keys"]`, or with the components): `mod` is ⌘ on a Mac, iPhone or iPad and Ctrl elsewhere. Never hard-code "⌘ D" or "Ctrl+D", and never write a product's own platform check, typing guard or key-label table.

- `onShortcut(shortcut, handler, { target, typing })` binds it, leaves it alone while typing (`isTyping(event)`: text fields, selects, contenteditables, textboxes and input-method composition), prevents the browser's action and returns the unbind. `matchesShortcut(event, shortcut)` reads one keydown; `isModKey(event)` is the command key alone, so Ctrl-click stays a Mac's right click.
- `shortcutLabel(shortcut)` writes it as the platform does: `⇧⌘D` (⌃ ⌥ ⇧ ⌘, no space) or `Ctrl+Shift+D`; the word keys are the shared words `key.ctrl` … in the page's language (`Strg+Umschalt+D`). `modKey()` is ⌘ or Ctrl. Give the control `aria-keyshortcuts` from `ariaKeyShortcuts(shortcut)`.
- Internal app-building hints belong to the private App workspace package (`packages/app`, `@0001-labs/app`), excluded from the public DS1 package. A hint is `<ds-kbd shortcut="mod+k">` (React `Kbd` from `react/kbd.ts`, copied with `kbd` by Sync 1), or `kbd.ds-kbd` filled with `shortcutLabel()`; `plain` in a menu entry. Arrows are written as arrows and set in Inter. https://designsystem1.com/1-root/keys/
- **Page inspection is on every page, for everyone**: five presses of G (each within 800ms of the last) toggle the grid and five of L the layout inspector (named areas, text and fields, components by tag name); Escape hides both, and presses typed into a field never count. A page loads it with `0-face/grid` (Sync 1 `root` `"modules": ["grid"]`, or with the components), which starts itself. Never put it behind a sign-in, an owner, an admin or a development check, and never write a product's own grid or layout shortcut; a product may keep its own touch gesture for its owner. https://designsystem1.com/4-page/grid/#page-shortcuts

## Tooltips

A tooltip is the system's label, never the browser's `title`: 16px high on 16px lines, 14px regular, 2.5px at the sides, 4px corners, black with white text (white on black in dark mode), two pixels above the element in the browser's top layer (`ds-tooltip`'s bubble is a manual popover, so no dialog, sheet or scrolling frame cuts it short), shown at once; `data-tooltip-colour` gives it a colour (an accent, a column's colour) lettered in the ink. ## Languages

Design system 1 owns languages for every product: the list, choosing and remembering one, the shared words, language-aware addresses, the Convex check and the picker. A product brings only its own words: the common buttons and actions (Save, Cancel, Close, Try again, Undo, Redo, Copy, Copy link, Remove, Rename, Duplicate, Download, Refresh, Open, Send, Clear) and Saved, Saving…, Untitled and Account are shared words, never a product's own; "Try again", not "Retry", and Close for a dismiss. The full guide is the Languages page (`website/src/content/docs/1-root/languages.md`, https://designsystem1.com/1-root/languages/).

1. **Import** from the `ds-one` package, or from the product's vendored copy of `DS1/` (Ezo's `client/design/0-face/i18n.ts`). Change the module in Design system 1 and sync the copy; a vendored copy is edited only by the sync.
2. **Configure at startup**, straight after the import: `configureI18n({ languages: [...], sharedWords: true })`. `languages` is the product's subset of `LANGUAGES` (`DS1/0-face/languages.ts`); the picker, the browser's language and the address prefixes all follow it. Where addresses carry the language (`/ja/…`), `languageFromAddress: true` starts in it without storing it. `langTarget` keeps `lang` on an embedded surface (or `false`, nowhere) instead of the page's root; `setLanguage(code, { remember: false })` changes the language without storing it.
3. **Words** live in the product's translations file, `{ "<language>": { "<key>": "<text>" } }`, keyed by the English sentence or by a dotted id with an `en` table. English is the fallback, then the key itself. Placeholders are `{name}` or `{{name}}`, filled by `translate(key, params)`. One file per language (`languageFile: "/translations/{language}.json"`) or a TypeScript module passed to `loadTranslations` work as well. Check a file with `validateTranslations`.
4. **Shared words** (Save, Cancel, Sign in, Nothing here yet, Today, the language names) come from `DS1/0-face/translations/`; use their keys before writing a product's own, and add a word every product needs there.
5. **The picker** is `<ds-cycle type="language">`: a normal button that fits its language name, one control high; legacy `compact` and `chip` attributes do not change its width.
6. **The backend** checks languages with `languageValidator` or `languageValidatorFor(subset)` from `ds-one/DS1/0-face/language-validator.ts`, imported by path in `convex/`.
7. **Dates, numbers and money** are written by `DS1/0-face/format.ts`, in the current language or `{ language }`, never by a product's own `Intl` wrapper or a hard-coded locale. The guide is the Formatting page (`website/src/content/docs/1-root/formatting.md`, https://designsystem1.com/1-root/formatting/).

Every text is sentence case, in every language.

## Formatting

- **Dates:** `formatDate`, `formatTime`, `formatDateTime`, `formatMonthYear`, and `formatMonthDay` and `formatMonthDayTime` without the year, each `short`, `medium` or `long` (`formatDate(at, "long")`). A date without a time (`"2026-10-17"`) stays on its day in every zone.
- **Relative time:** `formatRelativeTime(at, { now })` gives "now", "3 minutes ago", "yesterday" (by the calendar) or "in 2 days".
- **Numbers:** `formatNumber`, `formatCompact` ("12.3K") and `formatPercent` (it takes a fraction: `0.25` is 25%).
- **Money:** `formatMoney(amount, currency, { minor, display, whole })`. The currency decides the decimals (none for JPY), and `minor: true` reads an amount in cents. `formatMoneyToParts` is for digits drawn in equal cells.
- **Lists:** `formatList(items)`.
- **Locale:** `localeTag(language)` gives the locale (`da` is `da-DK`, `pt` is `pt-PT`). `configureFormat({ locales: { en: "en-GB" }, timeZone })` sets a product's region and zone once, at startup.
- **Servers:** pass the request's `language` on every call. `format.ts` and `pricing.ts` travel in the `languages` Sync 1 export.

## Constraints

The small rules every component keeps, each a sentence with a code that never changes, listed on https://designsystem1.com/1-root/constraints/ and on each component's page. Each is also a check of a page as drawn: on a Design system 1 page its owner presses C five times (a site with sign-in sets `<html data-ds-owner>` from its server-checked owner; a site without sign-in is switched on per browser with `?ds-owner=on`; Sync 1's root module `grid` or `constraints` brings it) for a side panel of what the page breaks; `?constraints=1` in the address opens the same panel for anyone, and its Site button (or `?constraints=site`) checks every page in the sitemap at 320, 375, 393 and 430px in frames and lists each failure with its page. `inspectConstraints()` from `0-face/constraints.ts` returns the same list to a script. Keep them in any 0001 interface, whether or not it imports the component, and name a broken one by its code in a review. When a general rule for Design system 1 is given, add it to `DS1/0-face/constraints.ts` with its `inspect` (the check on a page) and a source test, make the component keep it, then run `vp run constraints:write`, which writes the list below. Before releasing a site built on Design system 1 or a library, run `vp run constraints:phone <address>` in Design system 1. It opens every page in its sitemap in WebKit as an iPhone 320, 375, 393 and 430px wide, runs every rule, and fails while one is broken. A page wider than its window (`page.window-width`) is zoomed out by the phone, so fix the piece it names rather than hiding the overflow on `body`.

**Select** (`ds-select`)

- `select.hug`: Select and date-picker triggers use hug sizing, never fill sizing or growing to occupy the available row.

**Button** (`ds-button`)

- `button.visible-label`: Button labels stay fully visible, never faded, gradient-masked or truncated; round the required width up to whole grid squares. The action must remain readable; a chosen width cannot cut off its label.

**Text** (`ds-text`)

- `text.whole-words`: Headings and table headers keep each word intact across lines; only a long word that cannot fit may split at a valid language-specific hyphenation point, with a visible hyphen. Wrap between words or give the heading more room; never leave a final letter on its own, use break-all or anywhere, or insert an arbitrary line break inside a word. Use the correct lang with automatic hyphenation, or a soft hyphen at an editorially verified break; scripts with other line-breaking conventions keep their own typesetting rules.

**Color** (`ds-color`)

- `color.text-contrast`: Visible text, including muted labels, placeholders and avatar initials, has at least 4.5:1 contrast against the background it is drawn on. Measure resolved colours through slots and open shadow roots, including transparency and opacity; image backgrounds and effects that cannot be measured need a visual review.

**Card** (`ds-card`)

- `card.media-level`: A card's picture stands on the card's foot, a square below the words at least, so the pictures of cards side by side stand on one level whatever the words above them. The room a taller card leaves goes above its picture, never below it.
- `card.media-corners`: A picture is never rounded where it meets the card's edge: the card cuts it square there, and only a corner standing free inside the card, towards the words, keeps a picture's corner.

**Page** (`ds-page`)

- `page.window-width`: A page is never wider than its window: nothing reaches past either edge unless something it stands in cuts it off or scrolls it, so a phone never zooms the page out or slides it sideways. A phone widens a page that is too wide and zooms it out, words and all, so a piece one square too wide spoils every piece on the page; check it at every phone width from 320px up.

## Checking a design

Measure, do not eyeball: in the browser, read each element's rendered width and height and its position. Widths and heights are whole squares (minus the 1px correction for items), and positions fall on the 20px grid. A row that is 32 or 36px tall, or a gap of 12px, is wrong. Then the sixteen: in any row, a 16px icon's bottom edge sits on the row's bottom grid line and the 14px capitals beside it start 7px below the row's top line (measure the baseline with a zero-height inline-block, the cap height from `measureText('H').actualBoundingBoxAscent`); words centred in the twenty, or an icon centred in its square, are wrong.

Message compatibility: existing Notice/Toast APIs and native DS1Notice/DS1Toast remain supported. The assistant form uses the same message face with identity, content and actions supplied by the app; conversation state remains product-owned.
