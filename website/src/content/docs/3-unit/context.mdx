`ds-context` is the menu that opens where the pointer is: a right click, the
Context Menu key or Shift+F10, or a touch held half a second on the element
`for` names (or `trigger`). It opens at the pointer, or at the control's foot
from the keyboard, and keeps the menu engine's keyboard, typeahead, nested menus
and focus. A menu that opens from a button is a [Select](/3-unit/select/).

```html
<ul id="notes">…</ul>
<ds-context for="notes" aria-label="Note actions">
  <button role="menuitem">Rename<ds-icon type="pen"></ds-icon></button>
  <button role="menuitem">Duplicate</button>
  <hr data-ds-menu-separator>
  <button role="menuitem" data-danger>Delete</button>
</ds-context>
```

## The gap face

Context wears the face Ezo drew for its notes and board items. Each row is
black with white words in both themes, as Ezo's menus always were: in dark mode
the slate, darker than the page. Rows stand a pixel apart and groups two: an
`hr data-ds-menu-separator` between groups is a gap, never a line, and the page
shows through every gap, because the rows carry the colour and the frame only
cuts the outer silhouette with rounded corners. A group left empty leaves no
gap: a separator first, last or after another is quiet.

Every row is 19px with the pixel under it, a square's pitch, its words on the
sixteen and an icon at its end. A row the pointer rests on lightens, the
keyboard's focus rings it in the focus colour, a disabled row is dimmed, and a
danger row (`data-danger`, or `ds-button danger`) is red on either fill. Its
width is its content's, from four squares to sixteen (`--ds-menu-width` sets
one). Tokens: `--ds-context-fill`, `--ds-context-ink`, `--ds-context-hover`,
`--ds-context-danger`, `--ds-context-refused` and `--ds-context-radius`.

## Choosing, waiting and refusing

Choosing a row closes the menu after the row's own handlers ran, unless the row
says `data-keep-open` (a colour that cycles in place) or a handler prevented its
click.

While a chosen change is awaited, set `pending`: every free row is held
(`aria-disabled`, the wait cursor), the menu says `aria-busy`, and neither a
choice, Escape nor a press outside closes it. Clearing `pending` frees only the
rows it held. A change that was refused is never silent: `refuse(row)` turns
that row red for a moment (1.4 seconds), beside the product's own message.

## Long lists

For a long or changing list keep one menu: name the list with `for` and mark
its rows with `context-selector`. DS1 delegates the gestures to the row under
the pointer, including rows added later; `ds-menu-toggle` carries the actual
row as `detail.trigger` and the point as `detail.point`, and focus returns to
that row. Give each row its own accessible name. A plain left click stays the
row's; the click that follows a completed long press is consumed.

`openAt({ x, y }, control)` opens it from elsewhere (a title's own gesture, a
shortcut); `trigger-mode` is always `context`. For a gesture on a surface that
opens something else, `bindContextMenuTrigger` from `DS1/3-unit/context-trigger`
owns the same right click, keys and 500ms touch hold (`LONG_PRESS`).

## React

`DS1/react/context` composes it: `Context`, `ContextTrigger` (`asChild` keeps
your element), `ContextContent`, `ContextItem` (`variant="destructive"`,
`onSelect` may prevent dismissal), `ContextCheckboxItem`, `ContextRadioGroup`
and `ContextRadioItem`, `ContextSub`, `ContextSubTrigger` and
`ContextSubContent` for a nested menu, `ContextLabel`, `ContextSeparator`,
`ContextGroup`, `ContextShortcut` and `ContextFilePicker` (kept mounted
through the platform's file choice). The adapter owns React composition and
state; the element owns the face, gestures, focus and placement. Register
`ds-context` and `ds-select` in the browser before mounting it.

```tsx
<Context>
  <ContextTrigger asChild><li>{note.title}</li></ContextTrigger>
  <ContextContent>
    <ContextItem onSelect={rename}>Rename</ContextItem>
    <ContextSeparator />
    <ContextItem variant="destructive" onSelect={remove}>Delete</ContextItem>
  </ContextContent>
</Context>
```
