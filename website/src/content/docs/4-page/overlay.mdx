An overlay is anything that stands over the page. Design system 1 draws them all
with one component, `ds-overlay`, and a `type` that says which it is:

| `type` | What it is | Where it stands |
|---|---|---|
| `modal` (the default) | A question or a task that holds the page | In the centre, 20 squares wide |
| `sheet` | A phone's first choice for the same | From the foot of the screen, up to 32 squares wide |
| `drawer` | A panel beside a list, a detail or settings | At the `side` `end` (the default) or `start`, the screen's height, 16 squares wide |
| `detail` | Responsive details, access or settings | A drawer on desktop; a bottom sheet at widths up to 768px |
| `popover` | A small panel at a point | At `anchor`, kept inside the visible screen |
| `screen` | Folded navigation, a document viewer | Over the whole screen |

`ds-overlay` owns the behaviour and the look around a light-DOM `<dialog>`, so
labels, descriptions and form controls stay in one tree. A modal, a sheet, a
drawer and a screen open with `showModal()`: the browser supplies the top layer
and the inert page behind. A popover, and any type with `non-modal`, opens with
`show()` and leaves the page live.

```html
<ds-overlay open>
  <dialog aria-labelledby="example-title">
    <h2 id="example-title" data-ds-overlay="title">Example</h2>
    <button type="button" data-ds-overlay-dismiss>Close</button>
  </dialog>
</ds-overlay>
```

For a single question, use `confirm()`, `prompt()` and
`alert()` from the [feedback module](/1-root/feedback/): they answer with a
promise and are drawn as modals on the same native frame. Never the browser's own.

Set the host's `open` property to open or close it. `closeOnEscape` and
`closeOnBackdrop` default to true. The cancelable `ds-close-request` event has
a `detail.reason` of `escape`, `backdrop`, `outside`, `button`, `native` or `swipe`. Prevent its
default action when the application must decide whether closing is allowed.
Nested overlays retain the background scroll lock until the last one closes.
The lock fixes the body at its current offset, then restores its original inline
styles and scroll position, including when an underlying overlay closes first.

## React

Select `overlay` in the source snapshot to include the optional React adapter:

```tsx
import { Overlay, OverlayContent, OverlayHeader, OverlayTitle, OverlayDescription }
  from "./design/react/overlay";

<Overlay open={open} onOpenChange={setOpen}>
  <OverlayContent type="sheet" closeLabel="Close">
    <OverlayHeader>
      <OverlayTitle>Example</OverlayTitle>
      <OverlayDescription>Content and actions belong to the app.</OverlayDescription>
    </OverlayHeader>
  </OverlayContent>
</Overlay>
```

The adapter links the title and description, portals the content into the body,
and provides the shared close button. It does not import Lit during server
rendering; register the selected components in the browser before mounting it.
Use `OverlayFooter` for actions. `showClose={false}` leaves the close out when
the content has its own way back (a sheet's swipe, its own buttons); the header
then gives the title its whole row. A controlled root stays open when its
`onOpenChange` handler declines a request. The optional second argument is
the close reason, so products can tell Escape from a close button while DS1
remains responsible for the keyboard. A sheet and a drawer stay on the page
while they slide out, then leave.

## The frame

A modal is 20 squares wide, with a square of padding, square corners and no
shadow. Set `--ds-overlay-width` for a different whole-square width. Colours,
type and scale follow the shared `--ds-*` tokens. The title stands on its
square's lower sixteen, as every row's words do; the header keeps its last
square for the close button only when there is one.

`flush` removes the padding and gap when the content already provides its own.
Set `--ds-overlay-height` for a tall, bounded frame. Content layouts belong to
the application; the frame supplies its paper, type, border, backdrop and
behaviour. `rotated` turns a document frame 90 degrees and swaps its viewport
bounds. Applications decide when to offer rotation.

Menus inside a modal must stay inside its native dialog or use the browser's own
top layer; body-level layers are outside the modal's interactive area. Aa mounts
its floating toolbar, menus, find controls and link field inside the closest
native dialog. Sync Aa before placing an older editor copy in an overlay.

## Sheets and drawers

Phones take a sheet first; a panel beside a list takes a drawer. The modal
behaviour is the same in each: the focus trap, Escape, the backdrop and the
scroll lock.

```html
<ds-overlay type="sheet">
  <dialog aria-labelledby="trade-title">
    <h2 id="trade-title" data-ds-overlay="title">Buy</h2>
  </dialog>
</ds-overlay>

<ds-overlay type="drawer" side="start">…</ds-overlay>
```

- **A sheet** is as wide as the screen up to 32 squares (`--ds-overlay-sheet-width`)
  and centred beyond that, as high as its content up to two squares below the
  top (`--ds-overlay-sheet-gap`), then it scrolls. It has no line at its foot;
  its line at the top is drawn inside its first square (`--ds-overlay-line`),
  so its rows stay on whole squares. A handle two squares wide and 4px high
  stands in the middle of that square, clear of the title, and stays there while
  the content scrolls; `--ds-overlay-handle: none` hides it,
  `--ds-overlay-handle-color` colours it.
- **A drawer** is the screen's height and 16 squares wide (`--ds-overlay-width`),
  leaving at least two squares of the page beside it, with one line on the side
  facing the page. `end` is the right of a left-to-right page. It has no handle.
- Each keeps its square of padding inside the safe area of a phone's notch and
  home indicator (`env(safe-area-inset-*)`); give the page
  `<meta name="viewport" content="width=device-width, initial-scale=1, viewport-fit=cover">`.
- Each slides in from its edge and back out to it, in transform alone, while
  the backdrop fades (`--ds-overlay-enter` 380ms, `--ds-overlay-exit` 260ms,
  `--ds-overlay-easing`). With reduced motion it fades where it stands.

**Swiping it away.** On a touch screen a finger that drags a sheet down, or a
drawer towards its edge, moves it one to one after ten pixels, and the backdrop
fades as it goes. Dragged the other way it gives a little and pulls back. On
release DS1 projects where the throw would come to rest and dismisses when that
is past half its size and the finger is not moving back; otherwise it settles
home. Content scrolled down scrolls back up before the sheet moves, a strip
that scrolls sideways keeps its touches, and so do a slider, a range input and
anything marked `data-ds-overlay-no-swipe`. Dismissing sends `ds-close-request`
with the reason `swipe`: prevent it (unsaved changes, say) and the sheet
settles home. A mouse or trackpad closes it with Escape or the close button.

A drawer beside a list is often modeless (`non-modal`), so a press on another
row swaps what it shows.

## The screen

`type="screen"` covers the whole screen on the page's paper
(`--ds-overlay-paper`), with a square of padding (none with `flush`). It
arrives by fading in and settling half a square. `ds-header` and
`ds-page drawer` fold their navigation into one on a narrow screen, through the
shared overlay behavior. Focus stays inside, Escape closes it, and the page keeps its scroll position.

## Popovers

A popover stands at a point: set `element.anchor = { x, y }` in viewport
coordinates before opening. DS1 bounds it to the visible viewport with half a
square of clearance and updates after content, window and mobile keyboard
resizing. `element.place()` is available after a synchronous content change.
Products need no resize listeners or clamping code. Keep the accessible label on
the native dialog. A menu that opens from a button is a
[Select](/3-unit/select/), and one that opens where the pointer is, a
[Context](/3-unit/context/): use a popover for a panel that is neither.

## Modeless overlays

Set `non-modal` on `ds-overlay` to use native `show()` without a top layer or
inert background. Use `data-ds-overlay-inline` on its native dialog for a panel
in the document flow. In React, use `OverlayContent nonModal`; `portal={false}`
keeps the panel inside its product layout or containing modal. `closeOnOutside`,
`blockOutside` and `holdScroll` configure the same behaviour. A native `ref`
points to the light-DOM dialog. If the product removes its launcher while open,
set `returnFocus={() => launcherRef.current}` to resolve the replacement after
the closed state renders. DS1 still decides whether focus should return; a
modeless overlay does not steal it from an outside control. Set the mode before
opening. The shared stylesheet also installs into a containing shadow root, so
Lit products use the same frame without copying its CSS.

Modeless overlays can opt into `close-on-outside` and `block-outside`: the
outside click closes the overlay and does not activate the control behind it. A
gesture started inside stays inside. Escape closes only the top one. Set
`hold-scroll` to hold the page without fixing or moving its body. Nested modal
and modeless overlays release only their own scroll hold.

`await element.show()` waits for native opening and the first render of its
controls. `element.close()` closes and releases its hold immediately; camera
and media cleanup still belongs to the product's close handler.

Use `element.show({ immediate: true })` only when a rendered control must take
focus inside the opening gesture, such as a phone's search keyboard. The native
frame opens synchronously; focus that control before awaiting the returned
promise. Nested shared menus and inputs render synchronously too. When a new
input is added to an already open overlay, `input.renderNow()` exposes its
native field before gesture-time focus. Modeless Escape is handled after a
nested editor can cancel it, and keyboard scrolling inside the overlay remains
available while the page behind is held.

Configure a floating overlay with `--ds-overlay-inset`, `--ds-overlay-margin`,
`--ds-overlay-width`, `--ds-overlay-max-width`, `--ds-overlay-max-height` and
`--ds-overlay-layer`. Transparent media frames may set `--ds-overlay-paper` and
`--ds-overlay-border`. Use `--ds-overlay-overflow: hidden` when a bounded layout
provides its own scrolling regions.

### Responsive details

Use `type="detail"` for details that belong beside the work on desktop and rise
from the bottom on a phone. It follows viewport changes while open, keeps the
same dialog and focused field, and uses the visible viewport above the keyboard.
The sheet keeps the shared safe-area padding, scrolling and swipe dismissal.
For non-modal apps set `nonModal = true`, `holdScroll = false` and
`blockOutside = false`: the page remains interactive and scrollable at both sizes.
Keep anchored menus as `popover`, explicit side navigation as `drawer` and
full-screen documents as `screen`; those do not opt into responsive details.

Link’s people/access panels are the first consumer of this pattern. Other
consumers retain their chosen types until their own scoped migration.

### Small choices on mobile

Select menus and calendar pickers stay anchored to their field on mobile: centred
horizontally and exactly **4 CSS pixels** below the trigger (the gap does not scale).
Their rows and widths retain the design grid. At a screen edge they shift inward;
when below cannot fit and above has more room, they open 4px above instead.
Otherwise the available height is bounded and the choices scroll inside it.
Placement follows scroll, resize and the visible keyboard viewport, inside safe areas.
For a compact calendar in an Overlay popover, pass its field as `trigger` alongside
its desktop `anchor` point. Desktop placement and point/context/submenus keep their
existing rules. Clear on a calendar picker dismisses it and restores its field’s
focus; applications embedding a standalone month close their own context after
the clear has saved successfully (a failed save stays open with its error).
