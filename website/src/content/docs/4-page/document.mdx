A document page holds writing people read through or look things up in: terms, a privacy policy, a legal notice such as 特定商取引法に基づく表記, a help article. The contents stand at the start, twelve squares wide, and stay in view as the writing scrolls past; the writing, two squares after them, is a reading column of at most thirty-two squares, drawn by the [prose sheet](/1-root/prose/). Where the two do not fit side by side the contents stand above the writing and scroll away with it.

## Use it

```html
<ds-document>
  <h1>Terms</h1>
  <p>These terms apply when you use the service.</p>
  <h2>Using the service</h2>
  <p>…</p>
  <h2>Payments</h2>
  <p>…</p>
</ds-document>
```

The writing stays in the page as native headings, paragraphs, lists and tables. The element marks itself `data-ds-prose` and brings the prose sheet. Its contents list the headings at `levels` (`2` by default; `2 3` adds a third level half a square in), under the shared word "Contents". A heading without an id gets one from its words, in any script. A link scrolls to its heading and focuses it; the section in view is marked `aria-current="location"`. With fewer than two headings there are no contents.

| Attribute | Type | Default | What it does |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| `levels` | string | `2` | The heading levels the contents list. |
| `label` | string | `Contents` | The contents' title: a translation key, or the words with `literal`. |
| `literal` | boolean | `false` | Show `label` as written. |
| `hide-contents` | boolean | `false` | Leave the contents out. |

A slot named `contents` takes the built list's place. A part of the writing that draws itself takes `data-ds-not-prose`, and its headings stay out of the contents.

## A definition table

`ds-definitions` on a `<dl>` sets terms beside what they mean, as a 特定商取引法に基づく表記 does: the term eight squares wide in the muted ink, its definition beside it, each pair on a hairline at its foot with half a square above and below, as a prose table's rows are. Each pair may stand in a `<div>`. Below 480 pixels the definition goes under its term. It works inside or outside prose.

```html
<dl class="ds-definitions" lang="ja">
  <dt>販売業者</dt><dd>Name</dd>
  <dt>販売価格</dt><dd>各商品ページに表示します。</dd>
  <dt>お問い合わせ</dt><dd><a href="mailto:hello@example.com">hello@example.com</a></dd>
</dl>
```

Token: `--ds-definitions-term` (the term column, eight squares).

## Without Lit

`document.css`, which `controls.css` imports, draws the same page. The page writes its contents itself and marks the section it opens on.

```html
<div class="ds-document">
  <nav class="ds-document__toc" aria-label="Contents">
    <div class="ds-document__contents">
      <p class="ds-document__toc-title">Contents</p>
      <ol class="ds-document__toc-list">
        <li><a href="#using">Using the service</a></li>
        <li class="ds-document__toc-sub"><a href="#accounts">Accounts</a></li>
      </ol>
    </div>
  </nav>
  <article class="ds-document__body" data-ds-prose>…</article>
</div>
```

The contents column stretches to the writing's height, so `ds-document__contents` inside it stays in view; standing above the writing it is only as tall as itself and nothing sticks. Token: `--ds-document-top`, where the contents stop below the top of the view (one square; add a sticky header's height).

## In React

```tsx
import { DocumentPage } from "./design/react/document";

<DocumentPage levels="2 3">
  <h1>Privacy</h1>
  …
</DocumentPage>
```

The writing is the children, so it renders on the server. The adapter does not register the element: import `4-page/ds-document` once in the browser entry.
