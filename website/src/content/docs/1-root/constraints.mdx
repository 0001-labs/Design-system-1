Constraints are the rules every piece of Design system 1 keeps, so a page never has to be corrected by eye. They are measured in pixels and squares, not judged. Each picture below is drawn with the system's own pieces and magnified under a glass, its pixels ruled in and the rule drawn in blue. Move the pointer over a picture and the glass follows it.

## On the squares

Everything is counted in squares of 20 pixels, multiplied by `--sf` on phones and tablets (see [Scaling](/1-root/scaling/)). Widths are whole squares, heights are whole squares, and gaps are whole squares or half a square at most. Nothing takes a size of its own.

<Constraints example="grid" />

A piece that fills its squares is drawn one pixel short at its end and foot (`--margin-correction`), so two pieces side by side or one above the other meet on the grid line with a pixel of paper between them. Lines are one pixel. To see the squares behind a page while you build it, lay a [Grid](/4-page/grid/) under it.

## Fault lines

Fault lines are the continuous alignment lines made by related groups of cards and controls across a page. Their outer edges, column boundaries and gaps should continue from one section to the next. Two grids can each use valid spacing yet break those lines: a 20px gap in one and a 10px gap in the next shifts their inner edges.

The `page.fault-lines` check measures actual rendered edges and gaps across separate sections, including open shadow roots. Give grids that share tracks the same `data-ds-fault-lines="filters"` value. Use `data-ds-fault-axis="y"` for horizontal alignment lines through related rows; the default compares columns. Groups are compared at their current track count so coordinated responsive reflow is valid. Different card heights are allowed; hidden content and differences of at most one CSS pixel are ignored.

```html
<div data-ds-fault-lines="filters" class="view-cards">…</div>
<section>
  <div data-ds-fault-lines="filters" class="product-cards">…</div>
</section>
```

Unlabelled card/button grids sharing outer edges are also compared, but uncertain relationships are reported for review rather than failing a release. Name a related family to enforce it. Use a different family or `data-ds-fault-lines="off"` for an intentional independent layout, not to hide a genuine fault. A one-column Signal list, for example, need not share the two-column filter grid.

Run the check across the whole page at each viewport, not once at desktop size. The Site inspector and CLI now default to 320, 375, 393, 430, 768, 1024 and 1440px. Add widths immediately before and after the product's breakpoints with `--widths`; no finite sweep proves every possible width. Findings name the viewport and measured drift. The source regression reproduces mismatched 20px/10px card gaps, shifted edges, subpixel rounding and responsive reflow in Chromium and WebKit. Reach uses one family and one gap for its view, Product and Country cards.

## Nothing collides

Two pieces never stand on the same pixels. Rows stack edge to edge with no gap and no overlap; columns stand whole squares apart; a menu stands half a square from the button that opens it. What a piece draws beyond itself goes only where nothing else is:

- a pressed row's layer reaches two pixels past what the row holds, into the spare four above the next row's words, never onto them;
- keyboard focus is ringed two pixels inside a row, so the rings of two rows stacked edge to edge never touch;
- words that do not fit fade over their last square where they are cut; they never run under the next column.

<Constraints example="collisions" />

Anything a person can move lands on whole squares, and never on another piece. While it moves it follows the pointer freely; where it would collide is shown, and on release it lands on the nearest whole squares that are free.

## The sixteen

In a square, words, pictures and controls take the **lower sixteen pixels**, with the spare four above them. A 14px line of text is set on a 16px line box standing against the foot of its square; a 16px icon and a 16px control stand on the same sixteen. Centring in the twenty is wrong: the words would sit two pixels higher than in every other row.

<Constraints example="sixteen" />

| Where | The rule |
| --- | --- |
| A row, one square high | `align-items: flex-end` and a 16px line, so everything ends on the row's bottom line |
| A line on its own: a heading, a date, a label | 4px above it and a 16px line, still one square |
| A square with a rule at its foot: a table cell, a tab bar | 3px above the sixteen, then the 1px rule; never a 19px line |
| Prose that wraps | 20px lines, one square a line: the one exception |

## A list on the grid

A list is rows one square high, stacked edge to edge. Across a row, a picture takes the first sixteen of its square, flush with the square's edge; the words begin on the next square, so the square's spare four stand between them. Words without a picture begin on the square's edge. Numbers end on the row's last square in tabular figures, so a column of them lines up digit for digit.

<Constraints example="list" />

Lists, rows and their headers stand on the paper with no fill of their own. Colour is for a meaningful state, such as the open or current row, never decoration across a list.

## A face you add

The system's faces stand on the sixteen as they are drawn. A face you add, for a library or a product, has its own ascent and descent, so at 14px on a 16px line its capitals stand a little higher or lower: often less than a pixel, which is enough to make every row look wrong.

<Constraints example="fonts" />

Where a line box puts the baseline follows from the face's vertical metrics: in a 16px line at 14px it stands `8 + 7 × (ascent − descent)` pixels down, each as a fraction of the em. GT America's ascent is 1 and its descent 0.258, which puts the baseline 13.2px down the line, 3px above the square's foot; its capitals, 0.71 of the em, are 10px tall and stand 3px inside the sixteen at both ends.

To put an added face on the same line, give its `@font-face` the same difference between ascent and descent, and, if its capitals are taller or shorter, the size that makes them 10px:

```css
@font-face {
  font-family: "My face";
  src: url("my-face.woff2") format("woff2");
  /* GT Canon: ascent 1, descent 0.37, capitals 0.705 of the em. Its descent set to 0.258
     gives GT America's 0.742; its capitals are within a percent, so no size-adjust. */
  ascent-override: 100%;
  descent-override: 25.8%;
}
```

- **Read the face's metrics** from its file: the ascent and descent the browser uses (the `hhea` table, or `OS/2` typographic metrics when the face sets `USE_TYPO_METRICS`) and the cap height, each over the face's units per em.
- **Match the baseline** with `ascent-override` and `descent-override` so that ascent minus descent is 0.742.
- **Match the capitals** with `size-adjust`: 0.71 over the face's cap height. It scales the overrides too, so divide them by the same factor.
- **Correct the face once**, in its `@font-face`. Never move its words with padding, `top` or a transform in each component: every component then sets the face right, and the system's own faces are left alone.

### Correcting a face with the system

`DS1/0-face/face-correction.ts` does the arithmetic, so no product works it out by hand. Take it with Sync 1, `"modules": ["face-correction"]` in the `root` export; it comes with `google-face-metrics.json`, the measured metrics of the Google faces 0001's products offer.

- `correctFaces(css, metrics)` returns a stylesheet of `@font-face` rules, Google's css2 answer for one, with every face corrected in its own rule. Run it where the page is built and write the result into the head, so the first paint is already on the sixteen. A face without metrics stops it rather than stand off the line.
- `correctedGoogleFonts(href, metrics)` reads a css2 address and corrects it, for a page that chooses its faces while it runs.
- `faceCorrection(metrics)` gives the four declarations for a rule you write yourself.
- `readFaceMetrics(file)` reads a TrueType or OpenType file. `node scripts/face-metrics.mjs "Family"` measures a Google face and adds it to the JSON.

## Small rules

Under the big rules stand the small ones: a sentence each, kept by one component and measured by a test of its own. Each has a code that never changes, so a review can name the rule it finds broken. A component's page lists the rules it keeps.

## Checking a page

Every small rule is also a check that measures a page as it is drawn. On a page built on Design system 1, add `?constraints=1` to the address and a panel opens at the page's side once it has loaded. It lists each place the page breaks a rule: the rule's code, what is wrong in measured values, and the element ringed on the page. Press one to scroll to it, and **Page** to measure again after a change. Escape hides it. Under what is broken, **Rules** says what each rule measured and whether it kept, for example "Kept by 1,204 pieces measured on this page", or that the page has nothing for it to measure. The page's owner can also open it with **C pressed five times**.

**Site** checks the whole site the same way, at every phone width. Each page in the site's sitemap opens once, out of sight, in a frame that is then made 320, 375, 393 and 430px wide in turn, the page drawn at each phone's scale; three pages are checked at a time. Every rule is measured, and each one a page breaks is listed with its width, what is wrong, the element and a link to the page. When it ends, **Rules** counts what each rule measured over every page view, and **Pages** lists every page it checked, kept at every width or broken at which ones. `?constraints=site` opens the panel on that check. The frames' addresses carry `ph=out`, so analytics that honour it count no visits, and anything the pages store in the browser is put back afterwards.

The panel comes with the grid shortcut (`0-face/grid`, Sync 1's root `"modules": ["grid"]`), or alone as `"constraints"` (`0-face/constraint-panel`) for a site that shows no grid, and starts itself when the page loads it. Unlike the grid, five presses of C answer the owner alone; for anyone else they do nothing. The address opens the panel for anyone, since it only measures:

- **A site with sign-in** sets `data-ds-owner` on `<html>` once its server has said the person signed in owns it, and takes it off when they sign out.
- **A site without sign-in** has no owner to ask, so the owner switches on each browser they check it in, once: open any page of the site with `?ds-owner=on` added to its address, and the browser remembers it for that site. `?ds-owner=off` forgets it. The panel measures the page and shows nothing else, so a browser switched on shows no one anything private. A product whose `package.json` lists its `sideEffects` must name the copied `0-face/grid.ts` and `0-face/constraint-panel.ts` there, or a bundler drops a script imported for what it does and the panel never reaches the page. `inspectConstraints()` returns the same list to a script or a test.

### On every phone

A page has to keep the rules at every phone width, not only on the phone in your hand. When anything on a page is wider than the window, a phone widens the whole page and zooms it out, so its words shrink and it slides sideways. A piece that fits at 393px can break at 320px. The phone check opens every page of a site, as its sitemap lists them, in WebKit as an iPhone 320, 375, 393 and 430px wide. It scrolls each page through, then runs every rule on it and lists what each page breaks at which width:

```sh
vp run constraints:phone https://example.com
vp run constraints:phone https://example.com --widths 320,393 --pages /,/about/
```

It only reads the site, without cookies, so it is safe to run against production. It fails while anything is broken, so run it before releasing a site built on a library. The scale (`0-face/scaling`) keeps a page that a phone has zoomed out from getting worse: it never takes the page's width as wider than the screen's, so the scale cannot grow and widen the page further.

A new small rule goes into `DS1/0-face/constraints.ts` with its `inspect`, which measures it on a page, and a source test that measures the component; the component keeps it in its own CSS, and `vp run constraints:write` copies the list into the design-system-1 skill.

## Checking it

Measure in the browser rather than judging by eye:

- an element's width and height are whole squares, less the one pixel of correction where it fills them;
- a 14px capital's top is 7px below the row's top grid line and its baseline 3px above the next;
- a 16px icon's foot is on the row's bottom line;
- no two elements' boxes intersect, except a layer drawn over its own row.

The `color.text-contrast` rule checks actual rendered text, including component shadow roots, slots, input values and placeholders. It reports ratios below 4.5:1 and backgrounds or effects requiring visual review. See [Text contrast](/colours/#text-contrast) for scope and state checks.
