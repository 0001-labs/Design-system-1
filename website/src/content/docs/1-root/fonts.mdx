## Using the system fonts

`fonts.css` (`DS1/1-root/fonts.css`) holds the faces and the typeface tokens that name them: GT America Standard Regular and Medium, GT America Compressed Regular, the GT Canon trial, Inter Arrows and Iosevka Extended, loaded from `fonts/` beside it. `one.css` imports it, so a page that links `one.css` has them; a page that does not links `fonts.css` on its own. Neither loads anything from another host. Inter Arrows leads every typeface token and supplies the arrows.

Use `--typeface-regular` for body text, `--typeface-medium` for emphasis, `--typeface-compressed` for narrow display text and `--typeface-mono` for code, which is Iosevka Extended with a system monospace after it. `--typeface-canon` uses the bundled trial face. The component tokens in `component-tokens.css` (`--ds-typeface-regular`, `--ds-typeface-medium`, `--ds-typeface-mono` and the rest) name the same families, so either set of tokens finds the faces `fonts.css` loads. Check the original foundry's licence before using a commercial or trial font in your own work.

With Sync 1, `root` with `"styles": ["fonts.css"]` copies the sheet with its faces in `fonts/`; `fonts` with `"stylesheet": true` copies it beside the faces instead, pointed at them. `one.css` always arrives with `fonts.css`.

### Japanese and Chinese

The language tokens (`--typeface-regular-jp`, `--typeface-regular-zh-hans`, `--typeface-regular-zh-hant`, and `--ds-typeface-regular-jp` and its Chinese pair for the components) name Noto Sans first and then the reader's own faces: Hiragino and PingFang on Apple systems, Yu Gothic, Microsoft YaHei and JhengHei on Windows, Noto Sans CJK on Android and Linux. A page in Japanese or Chinese (`lang="ja"`, `lang="zh"`, Simplified with `zh-Hans`) sets its labels in that language's face. Without anything more, Japanese and Chinese text is drawn in those system faces and nothing is downloaded.

A product that shows Japanese or Chinese and wants Noto Sans everywhere adds `cjk.css` (`DS1/1-root/cjk.css`, Sync 1's `"cjk": true` on `root` or `tokens`). It loads Noto Sans JP, SC and TC from Google Fonts, which cuts each into `unicode-range` slices so a page downloads only the characters it shows, and stops the browser from thickening their one weight (`font-synthesis-weight: none`). Noto Sans is under the SIL Open Font License. A page whose security policy keeps other hosts out leaves `cjk.css` out, or serves Noto Sans itself under the same family names, which the tokens then find.

Olivetti Dot is available here as a specimen; it is not loaded by the system stylesheet. Supernatural has no font asset or typeface token yet.
