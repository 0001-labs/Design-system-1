Material is what a surface feels like. Choose **Solid**, **Paper**, **Glass** or **Wash**. Each keeps the same type, spacing and controls; only the surface changes. All four follow the page's light or dark theme.

<MaterialSamples />

## Four materials

| Material | Character | Where it fits |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Solid | An opaque, even ground. | Everyday pages, forms and dense information. |
| Paper | A warm ground with fine grain and soft fibre. | Reading, notes and quiet creative surfaces. |
| Glass | A translucent ground that softly blurs what is behind it. | A small, temporary floating surface. |
| Wash | An opaque tint with a gentle falloff of colour. | A sidebar, supporting panel or calm area of emphasis. |

Solid is the default choice. Keep paper subtle so the writing stays clear. Use glass sparingly over a backdrop with something to reveal. Wash gives a surface colour without sampling anything behind it.

## Apply a material

Load the optional stylesheet **after** the normal root stylesheet. It adds no JavaScript and changes nothing until a surface asks for it.

```css
@import "./ds1/one.css";
@import "./ds1/material.css";
```

```html
<section data-ds-material="paper">
  <h2>Notes from today</h2>
  <p>A place to collect a thought.</p>
</section>
```

The attribute paints that element's background. It adds no padding, border, corners, elevation or semantics. Compose the normal layout and controls inside it. A material on a custom element paints its host; it does not override a component's internal opaque face. Apply it to the surface that actually draws the background, outside a component's shadow root.

With Sync 1, add `"material": true` to the `root` or `tokens` export, or include `"material.css"` in the root's `styles`. A token bundle includes it when requested. Existing consumers keep their current surfaces until they opt in. These finishes are web foundations; Swift, Kotlin and email do not acquire them automatically.

## Adjust the surface

| Token | Default | Purpose |
| --- | --- | --- |
| `--ds-material-base` | `--ds-paper` | The opaque ground shared by the finishes and their fallbacks. |
| `--ds-material-ink` | `--ds-text-color` | The text on the surface. |
| `--ds-material-tint` | `--ds-sharp-blue` | The wash's tint. |

Keep base and ink as a readable pair in both themes. Set them on an embedded surface when that surface has its own colour scheme.

```css
.reading-surface {
  --ds-material-base: light-dark(#f4f4f4, #2c2c2c);
  --ds-material-ink: light-dark(#2a2a2a, #ffffff);
  --ds-material-tint: var(--ds-pink);
}
```

## Performance and accessibility

Solid is a single fill. Wash is one static gradient. Paper repeats a baked 128 × 128 PNG tile embedded in the optional stylesheet: no canvas, procedural SVG filter, animation or extra request. The grain belongs to the background and never filters the writing.

Glass uses one 8px backdrop blur. Keep it to a small surface; avoid full-page glass, scrolling layers of glass and animating the filter. Nested glass surfaces become opaque to avoid another blur pass. A browser without backdrop filtering gets solid automatically.

Reduced transparency, increased contrast and printing remove texture and transparency. Forced colours uses the reader's canvas and text colours. A product can also turn off effects for one surface or an entire subtree:

```html
<main data-ds-material-effects="off">
  <section data-ds-material="glass">An opaque surface.</section>
</main>
```

This attribute is an explicit quality choice; there is no hardware sniffing, timer or animation loop.

The separation of opaque, textured and translucent surfaces follows [Fluent's material guidance](https://fluent2.microsoft.design/material). These four finishes are Design system 1's own choices.
